## 2. Executive Summary


JORYKA needs a simple public landing page ahead of launch. The page must quickly explain what the product does, show how it helps retailers manage day-to-day operations, and make the next step obvious. The core message should position JORYKA as an offline-first retail POS, inventory, and credit management platform for live retail and wholesale operations where internet access may be unreliable.

The MVP is a single-page marketing site with a hero, five feature highlights, placeholder launch plans, a demo-request form or CTA, WhatsApp contact, and an existing-user login link. The design should feel modern, clear, trustworthy, and professional without relying on unsupported claims or invented testimonials.

Success means visitors understand the product, can reach the demo or WhatsApp path without friction, and existing users can find login quickly. Conversion targets should be set after baseline data or campaign expectations are available.


## 3. Problem Definition



### 3.1 Core problem


Before launch, JORYKA needs a clear public destination that explains its value to prospective Nigerian retailers and gives interested businesses a direct route to request a demo or ask questions.


### 3.2 Problem context


Retailers may need to manage sales, stock, customer credit, ledgers, payments, reporting, and staff operations in one system. Connectivity can be unreliable in some operating environments. The landing page must communicate JORYKA’s offline-first positioning without implying that every feature works offline unless that capability is confirmed.


### 3.3 Existing alternatives


Likely alternatives include manual books, spreadsheets, separate tools for sales and inventory, messaging-based coordination, and other POS or retail-management products. These are hypotheses for the marketing problem, not validated research findings about all Nigerian retailers.


### 3.4 Problems with alternatives


- Prospective customers may not quickly understand how JORYKA differs from a payment terminal or a generic business app.

- Fragmented or unclear product messaging can make visitors unsure whether the platform fits their store.

- If pricing is absent or confusing, visitors may leave before asking for a demo.

- If contact routes are hidden, visitors may abandon the enquiry.

- Existing users may be delayed if login is difficult to find.


### 3.5 Evidence and confidence



| Type | Current position |
| --- | --- |
| Provided facts | JORYKA is described as an offline-first retail POS, inventory, and credit management platform for live retail and wholesale operations. It supports sales processing, inventory, customer credit and ledgers, payments, reporting, and multi-user store operations. |
| Confirmed scope | Hero, key features, launch pricing placeholders, demo request, WhatsApp contact, login, responsive professional design. |
| Assumptions | Primary audience includes shop owners, retail operators, wholesalers, and people responsible for store operations. The public page is intended to generate leads before launch. |
| Not yet validated | Which retail segments convert best, actual pricing, final product availability, exact offline boundaries, approved product screenshots, contact details, and demo fulfilment process. |


### 3.6 Why now


The stated purpose is to market JORYKA ahead of launch and gather interest before the product is publicly available. No additional market-timing claim is required.


## 4. Product Thesis


We believe that helping Nigerian retailers understand JORYKA’s sales, inventory, credit, and offline-first value through a focused landing page will increase qualified demo enquiries and give existing users a clear route to login.


### Core value proposition


Manage retail sales, stock, and customer credit in one platform designed to keep retail operations moving when internet access is unreliable.


### Primary differentiator


Offline-first positioning is the clearest differentiator provided in the brief. The page must explain it plainly and avoid overpromising how syncing or individual functions behave.


### Product principles


- Clarity before decoration: explain the product in plain language.

- One primary conversion: make Request a Demo the dominant action.

- Local relevance: use Nigerian retail context and Nigerian naira where pricing placeholders are shown.

- Trust through accuracy: no invented customer logos, testimonials, metrics, awards, or guarantees.

- Mobile-first: core content and actions must work on narrow screens.

- Progressive detail: concise sections, scannable headings, and clear feature explanations.


### Reasons visitors may not convert


- Unclear launch status or product fit.

- No real pricing or plan details yet.

- Missing trust signals or proof of product readiness.

- A long or unreliable demo form.

- Unclear contact ownership or delayed responses.

- Unsupported claims about offline operation or data security.

### Recommended usability tasks


1. In five seconds, explain what JORYKA does based on the hero alone.

1. Find how JORYKA handles unreliable internet connectivity.

1. Find information about customer credit and ledgers.

1. Find pricing information and explain whether the prices are final.

1. Request a demo using a mobile device.

Positive signals: users can explain the product accurately, identify the main CTA, understand pricing status, and complete the demo/contact task without help. ## 10. Goals and Non-Goals



### Product goals


- Explain JORYKA’s product category and primary value in the first viewport.

- Present the five required feature areas with concise, accurate descriptions.

- Enable visitors to request a demo using a clear and functional path.

- Offer WhatsApp contact and a distinct login route.

- Provide a responsive, accessible experience across mobile and desktop.


### Non-goals


- Building or changing the JORYKA retail application.

- Implementing user registration, account management, checkout, or subscription billing.

- Publishing final pricing before approval.

- Creating a full multi-page marketing site, blog, help centre, or customer portal.

- Inventing testimonials, case studies, customer logos, performance statistics, certifications, or guarantees.

- Promising specific offline sync behaviour not confirmed by the product team.


### Guardrails


- No broken or dead-end CTAs.

- No pricing placeholders that could reasonably be mistaken for final prices.

- No inaccessible form controls or text embedded only in images.

- No collection of unnecessary personal data.

- No public launch until privacy copy and contact ownership are confirmed.


## 11. Success Metrics


Numeric targets are intentionally left unassigned until traffic sources, launch goals, and baseline conversion data are known.


| Metric | Definition / calculation | Window / target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Demo conversion rate | Unique successful demo submissions ÷ unique landing-page visitors. | Weekly and monthly; target to be validated. |
| WhatsApp click-through rate | Unique WhatsApp CTA clicks ÷ unique landing-page visitors. | Weekly and monthly; target to be validated. |
| Login click-through | Login link clicks ÷ unique landing-page visitors. | Monitor separately; mostly an existing-user utility metric. |
| CTA engagement | Visitors who click any primary/secondary CTA ÷ unique visitors. | Weekly; directional metric. |
| Form completion rate | Successful submissions ÷ demo-form starts. | Weekly; identify form friction. |

| Mobile conversion gap | Compare demo conversion rate by mobile versus desktop. | Monthly after adequate traffic; investigate meaningful gaps. |

North-star metric: qualified demo requests generated by the landing page. Define “qualified” with the client, rather than equating every form submission with a sales-ready lead.

## 18. UX and Interaction Requirements



### Layout and hierarchy


- Use a compact header with a clear visual distinction between the primary demo CTA and the Login utility link.

- Hero should include one clear headline, a short supporting paragraph, one primary CTA, and optionally a secondary WhatsApp or feature-exploration link.

- Feature content should be scannable; avoid long paragraphs or a wall of icons.

- Pricing should be easy to compare and should not suggest unapproved plan entitlements.

- Repeat the demo CTA after the feature and pricing sections where it helps the visitor act without scrolling back to the top.

- On mobile, stack cards in a single column or a compact grid only where text remains readable.


### Forms and feedback


- Ask only for information the demo owner needs. Suggested initial fields: name, business name, phone or email, and optional business type/message. Confirm required fields with the client.

- Use explicit labels, appropriate input types, and inline validation.

- Prevent accidental duplicate submission while a request is processing.

- On success, state what happens next only if the client can honour the promise. Do not invent response-time guarantees.

- On failure, keep entered values and offer retry or WhatsApp as an alternate path.


### Content and UX-copy principles


- Use plain, direct English that can be understood quickly by a broad retail audience.

- Explain “offline-first” in practical terms only to the extent confirmed by the product team.

- Use Nigerian context naturally; avoid caricature, slang overload, or stereotyped imagery.

- Use “customer credit” or “credit sales” consistently, and explain “ledger” in plain language if used.

- No fake social proof, exaggerated savings claims, “number one” claims, or guaranteed business outcomes.

- Use consistent CTA labels: “Request a Demo”, “Chat on WhatsApp”, and “Login”.

## 22. Roles and Permissions


Public visitors: read public page and activate links. Demo requester: submit their own contact information. JORYKA sales/demo owner: access incoming demo requests through the approved destination. Website maintainer: update content, URLs, pricing placeholders, and assets. Developer/administrator: deploy and configure the site. Permission rule: demo requests must not be publicly listable. Access should be limited to staff who need the data to follow up. Account deletion/export workflows depend on the chosen form or CRM provider and must be documented.

## 25. Accessibility Requirements


- Target WCAG 2.2 Level AA as the design and implementation goal.

- Use semantic header, nav, main, section, form, and footer landmarks with a logical heading hierarchy.

- All interactive elements must work with keyboard input and have visible focus styles.

- Use descriptive link text; avoid ambiguous labels such as “Click here”.

- Meet colour-contrast requirements for text, icons, controls, and focus indicators.

- Provide persistent labels for form fields; do not rely on placeholder text as the only label.

- Associate field errors with their inputs and announce submission status to assistive technology.

- Support text resizing and browser zoom without loss of content or functionality.

- Use touch targets around 44 by 44 CSS pixels where practical.

- Respect reduced-motion preferences and do not use animation to communicate essential information.

- Provide meaningful alternative text for informative images; mark decorative images as decorative.

- Keep copy plain and concise, and avoid dense feature descriptions.


## 26. Privacy, Security, and Trust


The landing page may collect names, business names, phone numbers, email addresses, and optional messages if an on-page demo form is used. The client must confirm the purpose, lawful basis/consent approach, retention period, processing destination, and staff access before launch.

- Publish a privacy notice linked near the form and in the footer.

- Do not collect sensitive personal information or unnecessary business details.

- Use HTTPS and a reputable form/CRM destination or a secured server endpoint.

- Apply spam prevention and rate limiting without making the form inaccessible.

- Do not send form contents to analytics tools.

- Confirm whether third-party providers process submitted data and disclose this appropriately.

- Confirm the applicable Nigerian privacy requirements with qualified legal/privacy support; this PRD is not legal advice.

- Use only approved brand assets, screenshots, logos, and customer proof.
